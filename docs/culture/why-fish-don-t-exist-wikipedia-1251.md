***Why Fish Don't Exist: A Story of Loss, Love, and the Hidden Order of Life*** is a 2020 [personal memoir](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memoir "Memoir") written by American science reporter and author [Lulu Miller](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulu_Miller "Lulu Miller") and illustrated by scratchboard artist [Kate Samworth](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Samworth "Kate Samworth").[[1]](#cite_note-:3-1)[[2]](#cite_note-:0-2) It incorporates the life and work of 19th century eugenicist and fish [taxonomist](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomist "Taxonomist") [David Starr Jordan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Starr_Jordan "David Starr Jordan"), exploring the search for objective meaning and order "against the chaos of the world".[[3]](#cite_note-:1-3) The memoir was published by [Pushkin Press](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushkin_Press "Pushkin Press").[[3]](#cite_note-:1-3) In 2025, it was longlisted for the [Women's Prize for Non-Fiction](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Prize_for_Non-Fiction "Women's Prize for Non-Fiction").[[4]](#cite_note-4)

Synopsis
--------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Why_Fish_Don%27t_Exist&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Synopsis")]

The book has a [non-linear narrative structure](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_narrative "Nonlinear narrative"), interspersing Miller's account of Jordan's life with personal anecdotes, including her experience with [depression](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_(mood) "Depression (mood)") and her relationship with her father, Chris Miller, who manages to find beauty in life despite his belief that it is [meaningless](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_nihilism "Existential nihilism").[[5]](#cite_note-5)

Throughout her memoir, Miller grapples with the conflict between her admiration for Jordan's relentless determination in his work and her research into his alleged involvement in the death of [Jane Stanford](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Stanford "Jane Stanford"), and his ardent support for [eugenics](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics_in_the_United_States "Eugenics in the United States").[[6]](#cite_note-6) Examining the psychological debate around "[positive illusions](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_illusions "Positive illusions")" and Jordan's fixation on the preservation of biological hierarchy, she warns against his dogmatic and certain approach to order, pointing to recent scientific breakthroughs that have diminished the existence of fish as a distinct evolutionary category as evidence that "nature is more boundless and bountiful than anything we can imagine".[[3]](#cite_note-:1-3)[[7]](#cite_note-7) Miller concludes, contrary to the beliefs of both her father and Jordan, that, whilst the universe is fundamentally chaotic, the subjective search for meaning is attainable, through our interpersonal relationships with one another and curiosity for the world around us.[[2]](#cite_note-:0-2)[[8]](#cite_note-:2-8)

Reception
---------

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Why_Fish_Don%27t_Exist&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Reception")]

In 2020, *Why Fish Don't Exist* was a finalist for the [*LA Times* Book Prize for Science and Technology](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times_Book_Prize_for_Science_and_Technology "Los Angeles Times Book Prize for Science and Technology").[[9]](#cite_note-9) In 2022, it was chosen as one of the novels for [Stanford](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University "Stanford University")'s Three Books program, on the theme of biodiversity.[[8]](#cite_note-:2-8) [Booklist](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booklist "Booklist") described it as "gripping, and sure to be on readers' minds long after the final pages" whilst [Folio Prize](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folio_Prize "Folio Prize") winner [Carmen Maria Machado](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_Maria_Machado "Carmen Maria Machado") described it as "a sumptuous, surprising delight".[[3]](#cite_note-:1-3)[[10]](#cite_note-10) Writing for the [Wall Street Journal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal"), Christoph Irmscher wrote: "leavened by a healthy dose of self-irony, Ms. Miller wields this familiar format with panache, spinning a tale so seductive that I read her book in one sitting".[[1]](#cite_note-:3-1) [New York Times bestselling author](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Best_Seller_list "The New York Times Best Seller list") [Mary Roach](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Roach "Mary Roach") commented: "this book is perfect, just perfect. It's both lyrical and learned, personal and political, small and huge, quirky and profound".[[3]](#cite_note-:1-3)
