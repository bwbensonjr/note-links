# Jurassic Park computers in excruciating detail

[Fabien Sanglard - WEBSITE](/)

  

Jul 13, 2026

Jurassic Park computers in excruciating detail

After I mentioned a Jurassic Park anecdote [the other day](../extinct/), I watched the movie again. I must have seen it at least ten times now. This time, I researched every computer/software I spotted.

**EDIT:** Just when I was putting the final touches on this article, I read the sad news that [Sam Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Neill), who played paleontologist Alan Grant in JP, has passed away today. **R.I.P** Sam.

Apple Powerbook 100

Surprisingly, the first computer visible is not on the island *Isla Nublar* but in Alan Grant and Ellie Sattler's mobile trailer. It is an [Apple Powerbook 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PowerBook_100), visible in the image below on the left side.

[![](00_11_15_Apple_Powerbook_100.webp)](00_11_15_Apple_Powerbook_100.webp)

Apple Powerbook 100

> It had a Motorola 68000 processor at 16 MHz, 2–8 megabytes (MB) of RAM, a 9-inch (23 cm) monochrome backlit liquid-crystal display (LCD) with 640 × 400 pixel resolution, and the System 7.0.1 operating system.
> [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PowerBook_100)

This machine's specs reminds me of how awful '90s laptop screens, based on a passive matrix, were. Definitely something I don't miss from that era.

Jurassic Park Control Room

All computers and software are located in the Control Room on the desks of two engineers, Dennis Nedry and Ray Arnold.

Dennis Nedry's desk is an indescribable mess with three machines (two macs, one SGI), three monitors, one PDA, and storage devices.

[![](00_43_24_Motorola_Envoy_2.webp)](00_43_24_Motorola_Envoy_2.webp)

Dennis Nedry's chaotic desk

Ray Arnold's desk is much tidier. It features a CCTV screen, storage devices, two computers (a Mac and a SGI), and two monitors.

[![](00_40_47_Thinking_Machines_CM-5_2.webp)](00_40_47_Thinking_Machines_CM-5_2.webp)

Ray Arnold's desk with supercomputers in the background

In the back of the Control Room, we can make out a giant screen and a supercomputer with tall panels and blinking red lights.

The book [The Making Of Jurassic Park](https://www.amazon.com/dp/034538122X) has interesting details about how they designed the Control Room.

> Everything in the set was real. We couldn't fake any of it, because audiences are so sophisticated now in their knowledge of computers. All told, $875,000 worth of computer hardware loaned by Silicon Graphics, $350,000 worth from Apple and some $500,000 in additional hardware and software went into equipping both the set and off-stage control room.
> [Cory Faucher (Special Effects Coordinator)](https://www.amazon.com/dp/034538122X)

This means, adjusted for inflation, Apple and SGI loaned roughly $4,000,000 of 2026 dollars for the production of Jurassic Park.

SGI R4000 Indigo

Ray Arnold's workstation is a [SGI R4000 Indigo](https://archive.irixnet.org/sgistuff/hardware/systems/indigo.html). It is barely visible in two shots. Blink and you will miss it at **54:48**.

[![](00_54_48_SGI R4000 Indigo Elan_1.webp)](00_54_48_SGI R4000 Indigo Elan_1.webp)

SGI R4000 Indigo (Hardly visible)

We get a somewhat better view of it towards the end of the movie thanks to a Velociraptor that never skips leg-day.

[![](01_54_56_SGI R4000 Indigo Elan_2.webp)](01_54_56_SGI R4000 Indigo Elan_2.webp)

SGI R4000 Indigo (Better view)

For the needs of the movie, that SGIs came in handy to run real-time 3D animation of the Hurricane. Or did they?

[![](00_40_35_Ray_s_SGI.webp)](00_40_35_Ray_s_SGI.webp)

SGI R4000 Indigo running 3D hurricane animation

> A dynamic and interactive method was employed to create the graphics, both on the big screen and on the computer monitors at each individual station. A makeshift room was built adjacent to the set, then equipped with a battery of Silicon Graphics and Apple Macintosh computer systems. Stored on computer disks were animations generated over a period of six months by a four-man computer graphics team headed by Michael Backes.
>
> Responding to cues received via radio from the set, Backes and his team were able to feed their graphics directly to the appropriate monitors on stage, making it seem as though the actors involved were actually calling up the imagery.
>
> [The Making Of Jurassic Park](https://www.amazon.com/dp/034538122X)

Gregory Gosson emailed me to elaborate on the animation showing on the Indigo.
> The software running on the SGI Indigo, showing the animated hurricane, was called Earthwatch, developed by Paul Douglas.
>
> The software was created so television meteorologists could create 3D fly-bys/fly-throughs of weather. The interface you see in the movie is the exact same interface the meteorologists would use when creating the animations.
>
> I worked with a local cable news station for over two decades. We used Earthwatch in the 1990s for our on-air weather graphics. The software allowed you to not only create the cool 3D graphics but also the 2D forecast pages, like the five-day and ten-day forecasts, and then to put them into a sequence that a hand-held remote switch would trigger. Basically, think of it as a PowerPoint slide deck. It was fantastic-looking stuff for the day, but the render times were very slow. The meteorologists would start the render sequence and then go to lunch. It could play the resulting animations in real-time, but could not render in real-time.
>
> Gregory Gosson  
> Former Broadcast Engineer for RNews/Spectrum News in Rochester NY

SGI IRIS Crimson

Dennis Nedry's powerhouse workstation is an [SGI IRIS Crimson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SGI_Crimson). It is such a beast that it won't fit on his desk. It is on the floor on the right of his desk (red box).

[![](00_48_53_chaos.webp)](00_48_53_chaos.webp)

SGI Crimson (red box on the right)

Most of the time it is used to display a 3D chess game (monitor the right end of Dennis desk).

[![](00_43_24_Motorola_Envoy_2.webp)](00_43_24_Motorola_Envoy_2.webp)

Chess, running on SGI IRIX Crimson (right screen)

The SGI Crimson is rarely visible on screen. It is briefly visible after Dennis's "white rabbit" lockdown brings Samuel Jackson into a depression.

[![](01_31_58_SGI_Crimson.webp)](01_31_58_SGI_Crimson.webp)

Crimson (also red box on the right)

The SGI Crimson was a very powerful workstation released in 1992. Its main appeal was its panel of real-time 3D graphics cards. The CPU was also very powerful with hardware Floating-Point Unit, a luxury for 3D graphics.

> * One MIPS 100 MHz R4000 or 150 MHz R4400 processor* Choice of seven high-performance 3D graphics subsystems (Entry, XS, XS24, Elan, Extreme, Reality Engine, VGXT)* Up to 256 MB memory and internal disk capacity of up to 7.2 GB, expandable to more than 72 GB using additional enclosures* I/O subsystem includes four VMEbus expansion slots, Ethernet and two SCSI channels with disk striping support
>       [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SGI_Crimson)

Like the SGI Indigo, the SGI Crimson was not connected to its monitor. The signal actually came from another machine in the makeshift room as pointed out by Ben Drago.
> I worked at Silicon Graphics in the '90s, and remember some internal presentations from coworkers who set up these systems for Jurassic Park.
>
> * The SGI systems on set used display extenders to feed the monitors from systems in another room. The Crimson was never powered on, as it would have been too loud. Keyboard/mouse extenders were used so the display was in sync when the actor was typing.
> * In the scene where Ray triggers the "magic word" animation, gr\_osview is actually running on a Silicon Graphics Onyx, and not a Crimson. The Crimson only had a single processor, and gr\_osview clearly shows four.
>
> Ben Drago

Thinking Machines CM-5

The supercomputer of the control room looks a lot like five [Thinking Machines CM-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connection_Machine#:~:text=of%20FROSTBURG%2C-,a%20CM%2D5,-%2C%20on%20display%20) with there characteristic front panel with thousands of red blinking LEDs. With a pricetag of "only" $46,000 per machine, it is very possible these were authentic.

[![](00_58_01_Thinking_Machines_CM-5.webp)](00_58_01_Thinking_Machines_CM-5.webp)

Four Thinking Machines CM-5

The CM-5, "**C**onnection **M**achine", was released in 1991[[2]](#footnote_2). In 1993 it was still considered the most powerful computer in the world[[3]](#footnote_3). Each machine was called a "node", featuring a Sparc CPU, four vector units, and 32 MiB RAM. As many nodes as needed could be connected together to form a mesh. The National Center for Atmospheric Research (NCAR) build a 32-node supercomputer with CM-5[[4]](#footnote_4).

[![](00_40_47_Thinking_Machines_CM-5_2.webp)](00_40_47_Thinking_Machines_CM-5_2.webp)

A fifth CM-5 can be seen on the right.

Does the red LED pattern in the front panel mean anything? Absolutely not, they were randomly generated[[5]](#footnote_5).

If you listen carefully you can actually hear Dennis Nedry talk about the CM-5, "**C**onnection **M**achine".
> I am totally unappreciated in my time. You can run this whole park from this room with minimal staff for up to 3 days. You think that kind of automation is easy? Or cheap? You know anybody who can network 8 connection machines and debug 2 million lines of code for what I bid for this job? Because if he can I'd like to see him try.
> [Dennis Nedry](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0107290/characters/nm0001431/#:~:text=I%20am%20totally,see%20him%20try.)

User pivo (from hackernews) explained why the movie features CM-5 while the novel has Cray [[debated](https://archive.is/6Pq4G#selection-445.171-445.199:~:text=didn%27t%20like%20the%20Cray%27s%20looks)] supercomputer.
> My wife worked for Thinking Machines back then. I remember that they'd asked Cray to loan them a supercomputer for the film because that's the computer used in the book. Cray brushed them off, so they turned to Thinking Machines who were happy to do it.
>
> To thank them, the producers rented a theater in Cambridge, MA [Kendall Sq. cinema] to screen the film just for Thinking Machines and I was also able to attend. By far the biggest reactions from the audience that night were when the CM-5 was shown for the first time and then when the young actress says, "It's a Unix system. I know this"
>
> [pivo (from hackernews)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48920798)

> I used to work at Oregon State University in their oceanography department where they had CM-5. I was friends with the technician who did the installs in the '90s. He had to leave part way to work on JP. He told me they only installed the front panels with the RED leds (not real CM-5 [[confirmed](https://archive.is/6Pq4G#selection-445.323-445.383:~:text=Thinking%20Machines%20lent%20Universal%20Studios%20the%20shell%20of%20a%20CM%2D5)]). Side story: When they were decommissioned, I rescued the LED panels and [rebuilt them](https://housedillon.com/blog/resurrected-led-panels/).
> William Dillon

Monitor SuperMatch 20-T

One of the very best monitors money could buy in 1993 was the SuperMatch 20-T. The twenty means 20" and T meant Trinitron. The SuperMatch was featured on the cover of [MacUser in Feb 92](macUser_feb_92.pdf). In [MacUser of Oct 94](macUser_oct_93.pdf), page 180 (out of 252!!), we can see it cost $2,589 ($6,000 in 2026).

[![](00_49_07_brands.webp)](00_49_07_brands.webp)

An amazing product placement for Apple and SuperMach.

20" monitors were considered absolutely massive in 1993 and only seen in professional workspaces. A typical PC would come with a 15" CRT. 21" is almost the maximum CRTs reached, their depth and weight made them very hard to move. They were replaced by LCD around 2005.

A lot of attention was paid to avoid showing CRT refresh artifact in the movie. SuperMatch had engineer [on-site](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25392870) and production had people dedicated to [syncing CRT framerate with film rate](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qicQUvSUbPM).
> My uncle (John Monsour) worked on this movie as the “24 Frame Computer Sync Engineer”. Because film cameras and CRT monitors have different frame rates, you needed to use specialized electronics to synchronize them with the camera frame rate otherwise you would have banding and weird moving artifacts on all the screens. It’s crazy to imagine needing to do this for all the screens visible in these shots.
> [amccollum](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48918088)

Sure enough, if we check out [Jurassic Park credits](jp_credit_24fps.png), we can see *24 Frame Computer Sync: John Monsour*.

gr\_osview

IRIX System Usage utility, named `gr_osview` can be seen a few times. It looks like a powerful tool, able to report not only user time, sys time, but also interrupt overhead and even gfx overhead according to [IRIX - Desktop User’s Guide](IRIX - Desktop User’s Guide.pdf) on p182.

[![](00_59_48_gr_osview.webp)](00_59_48_gr_osview.webp)

gr\_osview

Despite reports that monitors screens were faked via remote operators, `gr_osview` seems to react appropriately to keystrokes in the sequence above. Maybe this one was actually live.

White Rabbit (whte\_rbt.obj)

When Ray accidentally locks down the whole system, Nedry's face superimposed onto an Elvis Presley jumpsuit shows up on his Macintosh. That is the UI of the "White Rabbit", which Ray Arnold briefly mentions when he explains the lockdown to Ellie Sattler "White rabbit object. Whatever it did, it did it all. But with the key-checks off, the computer didn't file the keystrokes.". It is only in the novel that the program filename, `whte_rbt.obj` is revealed. Michael Crichton, the author of Jurassic Park, was actually a highly capable programmer.

[![](01_00_03_white_rabbit.webp)](01_00_03_white_rabbit.webp)

YOU DIDN'T SAY THE MAGIC WORD!
