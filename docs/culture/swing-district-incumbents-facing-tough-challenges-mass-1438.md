# Swing district incumbents facing tough challenges – Mass. Numbers

Incumbency provides a big advantage in legislative elections. Our [Massachusetts legislative model](https://bwbensonjr.github.io/election-modeling/) shows a 9-point lift for incumbent Democrats and a 16-point lift for incumbent Republicans, holding the other model factors equal. But incumbents in swing districts that lean towards the other party often face challengers and sometimes a loss. The 2010 cycle was particularly hard on incumbent Democrats in the post-2008 Obama win GOP midterm wave. Two Republicans State Reps that rode that wave to victory in 2010 face Democratic challengers on November 3 that may be able to to pull off hard-fought wins.

The four most competitive state legislative races in Massachusetts feature three GOP incumbents in Democratic-leaning districts with partisan voter indexes (PVIs) of D+3 to D+9, and incumbent Democrat Steven Ouellette (D-Westport) in his R+4 8th Bristol district on the South Coast.

### 9th Norfolk

The tightest race from the model’s point of view is between incumbent State Representative Marcus Vaughn (R-Wrentham) and Democratic newcomer Thomas Melville (D-Medfield). While the 9th Norfolk district has a PVI of D+7, it has not been represented by a Democrat since 1993, when Jo Ann Sprague (R-Walpole) led a succession of Republicans including her successor Scott Brown (R-Wrentham). Brown went on to the State Senate and then defeated Martha Coakley (D-Medford) in a January 2010 special election to replace Edward Kennedy in the U.S. Senate. Melville is running on a campaign of engagement and community service and raised almost twice the amount of campign money raised by incumbent Vaughn ($65,494 to $35,721 in 2026).

### 8th Bristol

The 8th Bristol State Representative district is a mirror image of the 9th Norfolk in that it has been represented by a Democratic representative for many years, but has a GOP-leaning PVI rating of R+4. Steven Ouellette (D-Westport) was elected for the first time in 2024 in a five-way race against Christopher Thrasher (R-Westport) and three unenrolled candidates including the current GOP nominee Jesse St. Gelais (R-Acushnet). The South Coast area has seen a great deal of back-and-forth partisan shifting in the Trump era. Challenger St. Gelais has raised 20% more money over the past two years and spent four times more than Ouellette, a notably large spending gap.

### 9th Essex

Representative Donald Wong (R-Saugus) has represented the North Shore’s 9th Essex district since 2011, after his 383-vote defeat of Mark Falzone (D-Saugus) in the 2010 GOP wave election. The 9th Essex district has a PVI of D+3, giving Lynn dentist and city councilor Pete Meaney (D-Lynn) a fighting chance in a year when anti-GOP and anti-incumbent sentiment seems to be high. Meaney was able to significantly outraise and outspend Wong, which may help to improve name recognition while competing against the well-known incumbent.

### 3rd Barnstable

Incumbent Representative David Vieira (R-Falmouth) also flipped his House seat from blue to red in the 2010 GOP wave, defeating long-time incumbent Matthew Patrick (D-Falmouth) to take office in 2011. The D+9 PVI in the 3rd Barnstable district gives Falmouth Select Board member Jack Richardson (D-Falmouth) a real chance, enhanced by the fundraising and spending advantage he had over Vieira during the election cycle.
