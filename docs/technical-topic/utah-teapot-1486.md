The **Utah Teapot** has been the symbol of computer graphics.
It was one of the first computer graphics models created using [Bézier patches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9zier_surface).
This is the official Utah Teapot page, where you can you can visualize and download any of these historical versions or customize it, using the settings above.

The Utah Teapot was originally created by Martin Newell and went through a number of modifications over the years. It quickly became famous within the graphics community after making its first appearance at SIGGRAPH in 1976. Its extensive use in graphics prompted Arvo and Kirk [1987] to name it the “Teapotahedron” and jokingly call it “the sixth [Platonic solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_solid)”. It remains one of the standard models for evaluating new computer graphics algorithms and frequently appears in technical papers presented at SIGGRAPH and other graphics conferences. Utah Teapot has also appears in art installations, movies, video games, and a wide range of other graphics applications. Versions of the Utah Teapot model are included in numerous rendering and modeling programs and graphics APIs, among them 3ds Max, Direct3D, Houdini, Modo, OpenGL, POV-Ray, Quartz Composer, and RenderMan.
See the [Wikipedia page on the Utah Teapot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_teapot).

[Martin Newell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Newell_(computer_scientist)), [Jim Blinn](https://www.jimblinn.com/), and [Cem Yuksel](https://www.cemyuksel.com/) tell the story of the [Utah Teapot](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/teapot/) at SIGGRAPH 2026, commemorating the 50th anniversary of the Utah Teapot's first appearance at SIGGRAPH in 1976. The extended abstract of this talk includes additional information:
“[The Return of the Utah Teapot](https://doi.org/10.1145/3799818.3816116)”
([PDF](The_Return_of_the_Utah_Teapot.pdf)) by Cem Yuksel, Jim Blinn, and Martin Newell.

Utah Teapot Versions
--------------------

![](images/utah_teapot_evolution.svg)

The Utah Teapot has a number of versions that were developed and released over the years by different people
from the University of Utah, as summarized below. [Version 1975](#v1975) is how [Martin Newell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Newell_(computer_scientist)) modeled it. [Version 1976](#1976) is the scaled down version by [Jim Blinn](https://www.jimblinn.com/). [Version 1987](#v1987) by [Frank Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_C._Crow) adds the round bottom. [Version 1992](#v1992) is the first attempt by [Hank Driskill](https://www.linkedin.com/in/hank-driskill-1a7140165/) for turning the Teapot into a solid object. [Version 2006](#v2006) by [Russ Fish](https://www.linkedin.com/in/russ-fish-b1113b/) improves the interior shape. Finally, [Version 2026](#v2026) by [Cem Yuksel](https://www.cemyuksel.com) further improves the interior, adds chamfers where the handle and the spout connect to the body, and makes the shape of the Teapot [curvature-continuous](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/continuity-enhancement/) to achieve reflection continuity.
